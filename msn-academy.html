01

The missing
learning journey.

The academy had a single-page website, but no student platform for learning after enrolment.

Our audit against the client’s requirements found a generic enrolment action, a course list without filters, missing pricing and FAQ pages, and legal links without destinations. Students also needed a dashboard, lesson player, assessments and certificates.

The design brief covered wireframes and UI. The internship portal was another team’s responsibility. We proposed a connected journey: discover a course, enrol, learn, complete an assessment and receive a certificate.

02

My work,
and our teamwork.

My contribution

I wrote the roadmap, created low-fidelity wireframes, presented them to the project manager, developed the high-fidelity screens and prototyped the desktop flows. I prepared the handoff and answered questions during development.

Shared with my teammate

We worked on the initial audit together. My teammate prototyped the mobile flows; I worked on the desktop prototype.

Developer contribution

Developer interns implemented a working demo from the design files. I did not build that demo.

I used AI tools to generate early screen batches, then refined typography, spacing, cards, content and interface states by hand. The project manager approved the wireframes before high-fidelity work.

03

Designing the steps
between screens.

The audit and requirements guided the design decisions. These were design proposals reviewed by the project manager, not findings from student usability tests.

  1. Give each task a clear destination.

    Separate course, pricing and verification pages replace a single scrolling page. A course catalogue adds filters and a direct route into course details.

  2. Make assessment states explicit.

    Rules and the pass mark appear before starting. A review step shows unanswered and flagged questions before submission, with separate pass and fail results.

  3. Account for payments that take time.

    Easypaisa, JazzCash and bank transfer options include a pending state, rather than treating every payment as immediate.

  4. Support certificate checking.

    A certificate ID connects to a public verification page with valid, invalid and revoked states.

04

A connected
web & mobile experience.

The design work covered about 60 web and mobile screens and five prototype flows. The dashboard, lessons and assessments share a consistent navigation structure.

Mobile layouts adapt the same tasks to a smaller screen, rather than shrinking the desktop interface.

05

What’s working.
What needs testing.

The handoff led to a developer-built demo. It demonstrates the proposed journey, but it is not evidence of student adoption or improved learning outcomes.

The designs were approved by the project manager, not usability-tested with students. Some assessment questions in the demo are placeholders, so that flow is only partly working.

Next, I would test course discovery and assessment submission with students, resolve outstanding client questions earlier, and formalise shared design tokens for handoff.

The project taught me to define responsibilities early, explain decisions clearly and treat pending, failed and review states as part of the core experience.
